Thinking about connections 
What is it?
It is a comparative report where you compare 4 texts and look at what connects them.
Select at least four texts. The texts can be any combination of written, visual, and/or oral and short and/or extended.
At least one of these texts needs to be independently selected.


What is this assessment?
Connections statement/idea
Text 1 
Text 2 
Text 3 
Text 4 
How do these texts connect with each other ?

How to connect your texts?
→ Identify your connections aspect. 
It can relate to: 
Style – includes symbolism, imagery, motif use, narrative perspective, visual and verbal techniques, and language choices.
Character – includes conflicts within and between characters, character change, minor characters that are used as symbols, characterisation techniques, and character relationships.
Structure – includes placement of events, editing techniques, repetitive designations, and lack of structural choices.
Mood – Does a similar mood prevail between texts? How is this mood created? What is the purpose of this mood?
Theme – Do the texts share similar concerns? Is there a similar director’s purpose?


How do I know if it is a good connections idea?
You have a strong connections idea if it has the following: 

For each connection determine whether you are able to:
form judgements about this connection
personally respond to the connection 
find specific details to support the connection
show how the connection reveals the wider context of the time
show how the connection reveals something about our society, our experiences, and/or ourselves.

How to connect in connections?
Connections within and across texts 
At level 2, you need to explain how your main connections idea is seen within each text and across all the texts 

The across text connections are where you get most of you grade. This is where you link your text to each other 

Within Text Connections 
This is where you explain how you connections idea is portrayed in each text (individually) 
→ You must use examples from the individual texts to support this 
→ Explain how each example shows your connections idea in that specific text 
This is where you explain how your connections idea can be seen in all of your texts 
→ This is done by comparing and contrasting examples from each text by talking about their similarities and differences in relation to each other, your connections idea, and creator’s purpose.

Introduction template (Example)
War is a tragedy, regardless of which side you are on. This is because war and conflict often result in death and make life harder for all people involved, regardless of which side of the conflict you are on. This idea is explored in 4 texts. Text 1 was is called Our Father directed by Calum Rhys, text 2 is called Soldier’s Home written by Ernest Hemingway, text 3 is called The Skies of Hiroshima, written by Hayashi Sachiko, and text 4 is called Coward, directed by Luke Walters. All four texts have a common idea. This idea is the harsh reality of war, and how it impacts people’s lives negatively.  Together these 4 texts show us, the audience, that whether you are a soldier on the winning side, or the losing side, whether you are young or old and whether you are in the midst of a battle or have already returned home, conflict will always make people’s lives harder and often forces them to face harsh realities.

BP1: Text 1- What to include:

 This text is called Our Father, it was directed by Calum Rhys. This text is set in the conflict of World War II. It tells the story of a young British soldier as he is struggling to survive and clinging to memories of home and family. This story shows how he watched as the rest of his fellow soldiers were killed and in his last moments, as the only solder left, he makes the decision to kill himself instead of being killed or captured by the German Enemy. My connections idea relates to this text because it shows the harsh reality of war and how it negatively impacts them by destroying their lives both physically and emotionally. 
An example that shows us this is seen at the start of the short film, when young Private Cole says, “I shouldn’t be here.” In this example he is reflecting on the simpler times of his younger life and explaining to his Sargent that this war is not a place for him, because he was not ready to be a fighter and was not ready for the harsh reality of being a killer and living under the constant threat of death. This example relates to my connections idea because it highlights how war tears people away from their families, forcing them to face fear and death instead of living the normal lives they once knew. Especially, as a young soldier, Private Cole was likely forced into the position and this quote shows us that he is not coping well emotionally and mentally with the harsh and gruesome nature of WW2.  Another example that builds on this negative reality that Cole is faced with is the low angle shot of Cole looking down at his hand. This shot shows that he had one round left in his rifle as he was being hunted by 2 German soldiers. This example relates to my connections idea because it shows us the harsh and complicated situation that Cole is in. This is because even if he managed to kill on the German soldiers there was still one more. This low angle shows us the hard choice that Cole had to make, and in this moment, this low angle is important because it shows us that Cole has decided to use his last bullet to end his own life on his own terms as his last act of control in this uncontrollable situation. This moment highlighting the brutal reality of war that it is violent and force soldiers into dire situations. 
Both of these examples are important because I think the director wants us to understand that war is not glorious or heroic in the way it is sometimes portrayed (especially in TV and movies today), but instead it is brutal, painful, and full of loss and sacrifice. The film reminds us that behind every soldier there is a human being with family, love, and dreams, and that war destroys those things in devastating ways.
